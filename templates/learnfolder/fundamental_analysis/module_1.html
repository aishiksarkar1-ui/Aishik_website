Module 1: Introduction to Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis is the study of a company's financial strength to determine the actual intrinsic value of the underlying business, rather than just looking at its stock price.

1. What is Fundamental Analysis?

At its core, fundamental analysis uses historical data to evaluate a company's sector and industry position, management quality, dividend history, and potential for future growth.

Example: If you are buying a used car, you don't just look at the price tag; you check the engine, the mileage, and the maintenance history. Similarly, fundamental analysis involves checking the internal "health" of a company before buying its stock.

2. Key Elements to Evaluate

When analyzing a company's fundamentals, investors look at several specific categories:

  • Financial Performance: This measures how much a company collects from customers (revenue) and how much it keeps as profit (earnings).
  • Financial Resources: A company must have the resources (assets) to invest in itself and grow.
