A Note Before We Begin

"To build lasting wealth, you must understand your cash flows. Use this tool to see exactly how your money works for you over time. Here is a quick guide on how to use it:"
  • Set Target: Choose your expected return rate and the future date you want to calculate your wealth for.
  • Deposits (+): Add the dates and amounts of any past or future investments.
  • Withdrawals (-): Enter the date and amount of any one-time withdrawal. The calculator reduces your projected balance from that date.
  • Add SIP: Set up regular investments by entering an amount, frequency and start date. You can also choose an end date. Each instalment is added on its scheduled date and earns the assumed return until the target date.
  • Add SWP: Set up regular withdrawals in the same way. Each withdrawal reduces the balance from its scheduled date, so the remaining money continues to earn the assumed return.
  • See the combined result: Your one-time entries, SIPs and SWPs are included together in Value at Target Date. Leave a plan’s end date blank to continue until the target date. These are planning entries; the calculator does not place investments or make withdrawals.

Cash Flow & Portfolio Growth

Follow three lines: your net cash flow, total investment profit and portfolio value. Net cash flow is the total amount deposited minus the total amount withdrawn so far. Each point shows the balance at the end of that calendar year; the last point stops on your target date. The first and last years may be partial years.

* Disclaimer: This calculator is an illustration. It assumes a constant annual rate compounded daily using 365 days per year. Each deposit adds to the projected balance, and each withdrawal reduces it from its date. Actual investment returns vary and are not guaranteed. The calculation does not include taxes, fees or inflation. Each withdrawal or SWP is checked against the available balance on its date. A plan is rejected if funds are insufficient; later contributions cannot cover an earlier shortfall. Deposits and SIPs on the same date are credited before withdrawals.
